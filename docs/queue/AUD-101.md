# `AUD-101` — AM DSP chain: off the DC spike, a real channel filter, AGC that is applied, and an in-channel signal level

[← Builder Queue index](../BUILDER_QUEUE.md)

🟡 **P2, second all-band row.** Filed 2026-10-11 by the Planner. Every fact below was read from code on `main`
`2a2893d2`. Nothing in this row has been measured on the box yet.

## Goal

Once `AUD-99` delivers MW and SW samples, they should sound like a radio: one station, not ±120 kHz of band;
steady loudness; no hum from the DC spike. The squelch, the signal meter and the scan should react to the
station that is tuned, not to the whole capture.

## Measured facts (code)

- **No IQ channel filter on AM.** `CalculateRates` (`src/RTLSDRCore/RadioReceiver.cs:1583-1612`) gives
  SDR rate = demod rate = **240 kHz** for every bandwidth under 225 kHz. AM is 10 kHz
  (`BandPresets.cs:23`) and SW is 6 kHz (`:55`). So `iqDecimFactor` is 1 and `_iqDecimator` is null (`:1444-1454`),
  and `AmDemodulator` (`src/RTLSDRCore/DSP/AmDemodulator.cs:36-51`) envelope-detects the **whole ±120 kHz
  capture**. At 10 kHz MW spacing that is up to 24 channels summed into one envelope. `_demodulator.Bandwidth`
  is set (`:1459`) but `AmDemodulator` never reads `Bandwidth`.
- **Tuned exactly on the carrier.** The receiver tunes the device to `_currentFrequencyHz` (`:453`). In
  direct-sampling mode, the RTL2832's DC offset and low-frequency spurs land on the carrier itself, and there is
  no NCO or offset anywhere in the chain. librtlsdr refuses hardware offset tuning (`rtlsdr_set_offset_tuning`)
  in direct-sampling mode, so the offset has to be done in software.
- **AGC is created and never applied.** `_agc = new AgcProcessor()` (`:157`) and `_agc.Reset()` (`:1554`) are its
  only uses. `ProcessSamples` (`:1711-1903`) never calls `_agc.Process`. The AM output level therefore follows
  the carrier strength directly, and with the 2.0.1 tuner standby in HF mode there is no tuner AGC either.
- **Signal strength and squelch are wideband.** `_lastSignalStrength` is the RMS of ~256 raw IQ samples across the
  whole capture (`:1714-1722`). Squelch (`:1734`) and the scan's signal test use it, so on MW every frequency
  reads the same band-wide energy.
- **The audio path relies only on the decimator's anti-alias filter.** The mono path (`:1865-1890`) applies
  optional de-emphasis (null for AM) and then `AudioDecimator` 240 kHz → 48 kHz (`Filters.cs:291-…`, cutoff at
  80% of each stage's output Nyquist, about 19 kHz). Nothing limits AM audio to the channel's ~5 kHz audio
  bandwidth.
- **AIR shares the AM path.** `Aircraft` is AM at 8.333 kHz (`BandPresets.cs:69-71`) and goes through the same
  unfiltered chain, yet `AUD-91` measured AIR "tuning normally". **NFM (WB, VHF) has the same missing IQ filter**
  at 12.5 kHz. That is out of scope here (see below).
- **Test seam.** `MockSdrDevice` produces an unmodulated tone scaled per simulated frequency
  (`src/RTLSDRCore/Hardware/MockSdrDevice.cs:303-…`). It cannot produce an AM carrier with a tone or an
  adjacent-channel interferer, so the tests need an injectable fake `ISdrDevice` that raises `SamplesAvailable`
  with synthetic IQ.

## Scope (AM modulation: the AM and SW bands, and AIR because it shares the path)

1. **Software offset tuning.** For AM-modulated bands, tune the device to `f − offset`. The offset must be well
   clear of DC and inside the capture, for example 60–80 kHz at 240 kHz; the plan picks the value and states
   why. Then shift by `+offset` with an NCO (complex mixer) before filtering. The receiver still reports `f`.
   Every path that tunes must use the same mapping: `SetFrequencyInternal`, `Startup`, `SetBand`, and the
   live-sweep `finally` that retunes to `_currentFrequencyHz` (`:1219`). ⚠ The sweep tunes its own centres
   through `LiveSweepTuner`. Do not apply the radio's offset there.
2. **A real channel filter and decimation.** Low-pass the IQ to the channel half-bandwidth (~5 kHz for AM, ~3 kHz
   for SW at 6 kHz) and decimate to a narrow demod rate that still divides cleanly to 48 kHz (for example
   240 → 48 kHz demod, so the audio decimator is 1:1). Either extend `CalculateRates` so narrow modes get a
   demod rate below the SDR rate, or add an AM-specific stage. Check the CPU cost on the N100 against the WFM
   path, which is the heaviest today.
3. **Apply AGC.** Run `_agc.Process` on AM demod output before the volume stage, with attack and decay values that
   suit speech and music on AM (the defaults were never exercised). Leave WFM alone.
4. **Audio low-pass for AM** at about 5 kHz, which also takes out the 10 kHz adjacent-channel whistle.
5. **In-channel signal level.** Compute the level that squelch, the meter and the seek scan use from the
   filtered channel (after step 2), not the raw capture. Keep the units and scale the API and UI expect today.
   Note any change in meaning in the XML docs, and check `AUD-91`/`AUD-100` code that reads signal strength
   (`StationListScan`, the seek stop threshold) for a scale assumption.
6. **Tests through the whole `RadioReceiver` chain**, with a fake device injecting synthetic IQ at the receiver's
   SDR rate:
   - an AM carrier at `f` with 1 kHz, 50% modulation → the output has a dominant 1 kHz tone (Goertzel or FFT bin
     check) at a level independent of carrier strength within the AGC range;
   - the same plus a stronger carrier 10 kHz away modulated at 3 kHz → the 3 kHz content is down by a stated
     number of dB;
   - a carrier at `f` plus a DC component → no low-frequency hum above a stated level;
   - the signal level for a tuned carrier is well above that for an empty channel next to a strong one;
   - FM (WFM) output is bit-for-bit or tolerance-identical to before on an existing FM test, to prove no
     regression.

## Implementation notes (Builder, 2026-10-12)

Built on `feat/aud-101-am-dsp-chain` on top of `AUD-99` (`main` `c61bd0d`). Anchors below are on that branch.
Box UAT (§ *On-box UAT*, as amended by the owner ruling) is still to run.

**Where it lives.** A chain of its own, `src/RTLSDRCore/DSP/AmReceiveChain.cs`, built by
`RadioReceiver.SetupAmSignalProcessing` (`RadioReceiver.cs:2042`) whenever the modulation is AM and the band's
channel is at most 20 kHz (`AmReceiveChain.Applies`, `RadioReceiver.cs:1901`): AM, Shortwave and Aircraft. Every
other modulation returns to the unchanged code below that line. `ProcessSamples` takes the chain once per block
(`:2230`, `:2279`). **Why not extend `CalculateRates`:** it also sets the NFM path, which is out of scope and must
not change; and the generic `Decimator` evaluates its FIR for every input sample and has no mixer, whereas the new
`AmChannelizer` mixes, filters and decimates in one pass and evaluates the FIR (SIMD, `Vector<float>`) only for the
one sample in five it keeps.

**Offset: 60 kHz.** The device is tuned to `f − 60 kHz`; the receiver still reports `f`. 60 kHz is well clear of
DC; the widest channel (AM: ±5 kHz, stop band from 7 kHz) then spans 53–67 kHz of the ±120 kHz capture, more than
50 kHz inside its edge. It is exactly a quarter of 240 kS/s, so the mixer is a 4-entry table (1, −j, −1, +j): no
rounding, no drift. After the shift the carrier is at 0 Hz and the device's DC spike at −60 kHz, in the channel
filter's stop band (and, after the 5:1 decimation, at −12 kHz, where the audio low-pass removes what is left).
**One mapping**, `AmReceiveChain.DeviceFrequencyFor(f, offsetTuned)`, reached through
`RadioReceiver.DeviceFrequency` (`:321`, offset while the AM chain is the chain demodulating) and
`DeviceFrequencyForBand` (`:329`, for a band whose chain is not built yet, by the same `Applies` rule). Used by:
`Startup` and its M3 retry (`:412`), `SetFrequencyInternal` (`:756`, so every tune, `TrySetFrequency`,
`TrySetBand`, the scans), the `AUD-99` M1 pre-tune (`:265`, next band's rule) and its re-tune after a refused
HF-off (`:283`), `SetModulation` (`:1187`, new: a modulation change can start or stop the AM chain, so the
device is re-tuned), and the live-sweep `finally` (`:1674`). `LiveSweepTuner`/`DeviceSweepTuner` tune their own
centres, unshifted.

**Rates and filters.**

| Stage | AM (10 kHz) | SW (6 kHz) | AIR (8.333 kHz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDR rate | 240 kS/s | 240 kS/s | 240 kS/s |
| Channel FIR (Hamming sinc, at 240 kS/s): −6 dB cutoff = half-bandwidth | 5.0 kHz | 3.0 kHz | 4.17 kHz |
| Transition = 0.4 × bandwidth; taps = ⌈3.3·fs/Δf⌉, odd | 4 kHz → **199 taps** | 2.4 kHz → **331** | 3.33 kHz → **239** |
| Decimation | 5:1 → 48 kHz (no audio decimator) | same | same |
| Audio low-pass (Hamming, 48 kHz), cutoff = half-bandwidth, 1.5 kHz transition | 5 kHz, 107 taps | **3 kHz**, 107 taps | 4.17 kHz, 107 taps |

Shortwave gets the narrower 3 kHz audio low-pass because SW broadcasters sit 5 kHz apart: the adjacent carrier's
whistle is at 5 kHz, inside a 5 kHz low-pass's transition band but in a 3 kHz one's stop band. The channel filter
already limits SW audio to about 3 kHz, so nothing audible is lost. Order: channel → envelope (DC blocker, 41.7 ms,
seeded from the first block's mean) → audio low-pass → AGC → volume.

**AGC.** `AgcProcessor` was not just unused, it was broken: it followed the level of its own *output*, so each
gain produced the opposite correction on the next sample. It now follows the input envelope (feed-forward) and is
seeded from the first block (√2 × RMS). Parameters on AM: **target 0.5** (output peak, −6 dBFS), **attack 5 ms**,
**decay 1 s**, **max gain 30** (+29.5 dB), min gain 0.01. The decay is slow enough not to pump on speech and music;
the max gain holds an empty channel's hiss down (box noise: about −26 dBFS RMS, from the synthetic test at the
measured 0.02 raw RMS) and gives full AGC for in-channel carriers above about −30 dBFS. The DC blocker and the AGC
are re-seeded on the first block after each tune (a tune counter checked on the DSP thread), so a retune neither
comes out as a DC step nor waits out the old station's 1 s decay.

**In-channel level: the field to read.** On AM, SW and AIR, `RadioReceiver.SignalStrength` is
`max(0, (L + 60) / 60)` with **L = 10·log10(mean |y|²)** over the filtered, decimated channel of the last IQ block
(about 3,277 samples, 68 ms), in dBFS (full-scale IQ = 0 dB). It is updated once per block, unsmoothed. Through
the API: `signalStrength = clamp((int)(100·s), 0, 100)` and
**`rssiDbu = −60 + 0.6 × signalStrength` ≈ L, truncated down to a 0.6 dB step** (floor −60, ceiling 0). **Read
`rssiDbu` in `GET /api/radio/state`.** In the tests an unmodulated carrier of amplitude A reads 20·log10(A) to
±0.5 dB and a 1 dB step reads 1.0 ± 0.1 dB; per-block noise on an empty channel is about ±0.2 dB, so average 10 or
more reads per channel (≈1 s), and wait about 0.5 s after each tune for queued blocks to drain. Expected on the box
for an empty channel (raw RMS 0.02): about **−48 dBFS on AM, −50 on SW**. FM, WB and VHF keep the old wideband
RMS (linear) scale. *Why the scale changed on these bands:* the linear scale put the whole AM band at 1–2 % on the
box (`rssiDbu` −59.4/−58.8, `AUD-99`'s night check), so a 1 dB difference was invisible.

**Consumers checked.** The squelch, the meter, `SeekStationFound` and the seek stop test all read
`SignalStrength`. `StationListScan` (`AUD-100`) measures nothing. `RecordSeekStation` drops AM and SW (no
sweep plan below 24 MHz), so on AM-modulated bands only AIR records the new scale. The seek stop threshold
(`RadioOptions.ScanStopThreshold`, default 50 %) now means in-channel −30 dBFS on these bands. ⚠ **Not measured:
AIR seek on noise.** With the tuner's AGC, an empty AIR channel's in-channel level is unknown; if it reads at or
above −30 dBFS (`rssiDbu` ≥ −30), AIR seek will stop on every channel. Check `rssiDbu` on an empty AIR channel
during the UAT.

**Squelch.** AM and SW: still bypassed (`SquelchApplies`, `AUD-99` S1; the comment now says why it stays so). AIR
keeps its squelch, now on the in-channel level. The 0.1 default sits at −54 dBFS on that scale; white noise that
opened the old wideband squelch (raw RMS ≥ 0.1, −20 dBFS) is at least −35 dBFS in the 8.3 kHz channel, so AIR does
not newly mute (test: raw noise 0.12 reads strength 0.45 ≥ 0.1), while an empty, quiet channel (raw −60 dBFS)
still squelches.

**CPU.** Method: a throwaway harness (not committed) fed 150 blocks (10.24 s of IQ) of synthetic signal through
the whole `RadioReceiver` on the dev box (Windows, x64, Release), timing the DSP thread's rendezvous, synthesis
included (36–56 ms of it). **FM (WFM stereo + RDS): 1,457 ms. AM: 138 ms. SW: 139 ms. AIR: 97 ms.** The AM-family
chain costs about a tenth of FM, and less than the old AM path (whose 5:1 audio decimator ran a 165-tap FIR on every
240 kHz sample): channel FIR 199 taps × 2 × 48 kHz ≈ 19 M MAC/s (SIMD), audio low-pass 107 × 48 kHz ≈ 5 M; against
FM's audio decimators alone at 165 × 240 kHz × 2 ≈ 79 M MAC/s, scalar. Box check: `top -b -n1 | head -15` on
`radio-api`, AM playing vs FM playing.

device was actually tuned to, one block at a time with a rendezvous on each block's audio event, nothing timed).

  −5.85 dBFS]; the level is in [−9, −4] dBFS.

**Mutation checks** (each on the committed tree, reverted with `git checkout`): channel filter removed → (b) fails
(3 kHz at **+14.8 dB** over the tone), and (c), (d), (g) fail too; AGC removed → (a) fails (−33.95 vs −13.95 dBFS);

**Found on the way, not changed.**
- A custom-band tune (`TrySetFrequency` outside every preset) never re-runs `SetupSignalProcessing` and keeps the
  previous modulation, so from AM it still demodulates AM, now with the offset (the AUD-99 test was updated to say
  so). This predates `AUD-101`.
- `Decimator.Decimate` stops reading its input when the output buffer fills, which would drop samples and shift
  the phase. Today no path hits it (the IQ decimator is unused: every rate pair is 1:1); the new channelizer
  consumes every sample and checks the buffer size instead.
- `AmDemodulator.DcBlockerTimeConstant` was read only in the constructor, so setting it did nothing; it is replaced
  by `DcBlockerTimeConstantSeconds`.
- `MockSdrDevice` puts its 1 kHz tone at the device centre, so on AM with the offset the mock plays noise.
